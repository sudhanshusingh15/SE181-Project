3. Assumptions where the plan is silent

  1. Window arithmetic. Day 1 of F1-30D is the first worked shift itself, so the windows are the first worked date plus 0 to 29, 30 to 59 and 60 to 89 days. Recommend writing the day count into the plan.
  2. GTM shifts posted before the window opened. They fall in F1-30D (7.6) but no earlier statement could have recognised them. I recognise them on the first statement where the window exists. The alternative moves $915. Recommend codifying one treatment.
  3. Employment test (7.11). F1-30D revenue is tested by worked date (posted date for GTM); window improvements by the window close date. Katniss Everdeen is paid for shifts worked through 12 June, $230.
  4. Manager roll-up. Managers carry the full June revenue of every account beneath them, including what a leaver or late joiner is not paid, after each report's ramp floor (7.8). The floor itself counts as revenue carried.
  5. Ramp restart on a lateral move. SMB AE to ENT AE on 1 May is treated as a promotion into a ramped role, so the clock restarts (7.10). If leadership reads it as lateral, Scout Finch gains $339 of accelerators and nothing else changes.
  6. Credits on accounts not yet existing reduce nobody's number this month, rather than the future owner's.
  7. Retention SPIFF book. Accounts live before 1 May and held in June, measured in both months net of each month's approved credits; ramping AMs excluded even where the book held. Dr. Watson's book came in at 89.7% of May ($206,856 against $230,674), so no SPIFF.
  8. Quota for role changers. No proration for a role change (7.11): Anna carries the full ENT AM quota for June.
  9. Currency. The tab 3 rate is a single static rate; I recommend the plan name the strike date. Puerto Rico pays in USD at 1.00.
  10. Opportunity stage governs. Residue rows are ignored, go-live is the earliest Closed Won, and an expansion in June does not restart an account.

Assumptions: bands unchanged at $30k to $40k and $40k+; ramping reps stay ineligible; managers are unaffected because they are paid on credited revenue, not on reps' commission; nothing else in June changes. What surprised me: the change costs about 1% of SMB AE commission because only 13 of 28 SMB AEs clear $30k and are eligible, and the three ramping reps are excluded, including Gimli at 120% of quota. Band I only spans $10k, so the 1.3x step is worth at most $160 to any rep; the money sits in band II, which is where the one payout I want to hold also sits. If the goal is to reward the top third, widening band II or raising the multiplier there does more than touching band I.

Note to sales leadership (248 words)

Subject: Hold on one GTM account before June statements are final

I am holding new-logo credit on one account and need your decision by Thursday to keep the payroll date.

The account went live on 1 June as a GTM account, closed by Jon Snow. In June the facility posted 313 shifts and 45 were worked, a 14 percent fill rate. The other 39 GTM accounts fill 73 percent, the same as Standard accounts. Ops flagged the pattern, and Forrest Gump spoke to the facility: they expect a handful of shifts a day and do not know where the volume came from. Jon told #sales-ops on 15 June that he has been loading shift templates for rural sites himself.

GTM credits gross value on posted shifts, so the plan as written credits $41,081 of revenue for this one account. That takes Jon from 15 percent of quota to 152 percent and from $721 of commission to $7,883. His manager chain picks up another $2,177. Net revenue actually earned on the account in June was $8,100.

My recommendation: hold the $9,339 until Ops confirms what the facility asked for. If the posts were in good faith, pay on worked shifts only. If they were not, pay nothing on this account and treat it as a conduct matter. From July, add a guardrail: GTM posted volume credits only up to a fill-rate floor, and Ops reviews any account that fills under half of what it posts before statements go out.
